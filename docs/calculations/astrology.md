# Astrology

The zodiac divides longitude into twelve equal 30° segments. Tropical starts at the March equinox. Sidereal Lahiri and Fagan–Bradley subtract their mean ayanamsas from the apparent tropical longitude. The mean offsets use the published epoch anchors and IAU 2006 general precession in longitude. Apparent nutation and differences between precession models can move a displayed position slightly near a sign boundary. The same zodiac selection applies to sign labels, wheel positions, ingresses, and widget snapshots. [IAU precession model](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2005/10/aa1908.pdf), [Lahiri and Fagan–Bradley anchors](https://www.astro.com/swisseph-download/doc/swisseph.pdf)

The Planets detail dialog searches 183 days either side of the selected instant for the previous and next ingress and direct and retrograde stations. The solver samples motion daily, splits intervals at a reversal to catch both sign crossings, and refines each bracket to one second. That is solver resolution. The physical accuracy depends on the ephemeris. The March–April 2025 Mercury retrograde interval is checked against JPL Horizons hourly observer ecliptic longitudes (body 199, centre 500@399, quantity 31), allowing 30 minutes for the coarse reference. [Horizons API](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html)

The wheel has a text companion listing sign positions and active aspects. Aspects compare the smaller angular separation with 0°, 60°, 90°, 120°, and 180°. One configurable orb applies to all five. Applying and separating depend on the instantaneous relative longitude rate, with 0.001° tolerance for exactness. Selecting an active aspect searches seven days either side of the selected instant, with hourly brackets and one-second refinement. A common zodiac offset cancels out of pairwise longitude differences.

Traditional associations appear as interpretations. Personal astrology should make houses and angles conditional on birth time and latitude.
